# Positioning Framework & Statement · Roamly Groups

## 1. Positioning framework

- **For (target audience):** The friend in the group chat who suggests one local activity, such as the kayak on Saturday, and wants a few friends there without becoming the person who runs it.
- **Who want / need:** A way to turn that suggestion into a booked plan without resending the link, moving the time because one person can't make it, or chasing each payment. Once they suggest it, they want to stop being in charge.
- **We offer:** Roamly Groups, a group booking for one local activity with friends. One person sends the invitation. Each friend accepts their own spot, changes or drops that spot themselves, and pays on their own card.
- **Because of (value proposition):** Each friend handles their own spot, their own change, and their own payment. The person who suggested the activity brings the group together and stops being its administrator.
- **Despite (barriers):** Roamly is new to group booking, and every friend has to join and pay inside it. If they do not, the suggester is back to sending links and chasing payments. The host still sets the price, the number of spots, and the cancellation rules, so a friend cannot always change or drop a spot freely.
- **Better than (differentiation):** One booking, instead of a booking page, a group chat, a poll, and a payment app used side by side. The activity stays in one place, each friend holds their own spot and their own card, and the person who suggested it steps out of the changes and the payments.

## 2. Positioning statement

### First draft
Roamly Groups is a group booking for one local activity with friends: one person sends the invitation, and each friend accepts their own spot, changes or drops it, and pays on their own card. The person who suggested the activity brings the group together and then steps out of the changes and the payments.

### Pressure test
- **Test I ran:** A · Audience recognition
- **What it revealed:** **What the test revealed**
The reader recognized the job in the last sentence. They are the person who says "kayaks Saturday" and wants a few friends to show up, without holding card numbers, schedule changes, or payment requests. What landed was the handoff: they bring the group together, then each person takes, changes, or drops their own spot and pays on their own card. They heard the right situation, one local activity on one Saturday, not a trip and not a club. They still want to see who is in, and the statement leaves that with them.

**Where it felt too broad, generic, or unclear**
Two phrases sit off how they actually do this. "Invitations" assumes they send an invitation. They drop the plan in the chat they already have. "Group booking," and the opening "planning and booking service," sound like a product category before they sound like Saturday. That stiffness fades once the sentence says what they stop doing. "Shared itinerary" and "curated local experiences" also read wider than the job they named: one activity, one Saturday. The feature list is broader than the part they cared about, which was changes and payments leaving their hands.

### Final statement
Roamly Groups coordinates one local activity with friends. The person who starts the plan brings the group together, and each person takes their own spot, changes it, drops it, and pays on their own card.
