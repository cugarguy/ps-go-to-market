# Competitive Intelligence & Strategic Bet · Roamly Groups

## 1. Primary competitor

- **Competitor:** Airbnb Experiences
- **Core offering:** Airbnb’s core offering is a marketplace for stays, experiences, and services with Local-expert-led experiences, bookable individually or as a private group.
- **How they position themselves:** Discover distinctive experiences through local expertise, with private-group options where hosts offer them. Airbnb also supports guest invitations and group messaging after booking. Experience offering, group-booking details.
- **Primary audience:** Travelers seeking experiences led by local experts, including organizers of family trips, gatherings, and work outings.
- **Known pricing or packaging:** Variable listing prices; private groups subject to a host-set minimum. Airbnb typically takes a 20% host commission. Reservation sharing and group messaging are included in the booking workflow.
- **Why they're the biggest threat:** A compelling experience plus a familiar travel environment. For coordinators already using Airbnb, booking an activity there can feel like a natural extension. Private groups, host customization, and shared messages already address part of your promise.

## 2. Battlecard

### Competitive Battlecard: Airbnb Experiences
**Product:** Roamly Groups  
**Target buyer:** Individual travelers coordinating a shared local experience  
**Updated:** November 4, 2026  
**Status:** Working battlecard; comparative outcomes require validation

> **Competitive takeaway:** Airbnb competes strongly on the experience itself and offers private-group booking and communication tools. Roamly’s opportunity is to make agreement, split payments, and booking work together—especially for existing Roamly customers.

#### 1. Know the Landscape

##### Competitor Overview
Airbnb Experiences connects travelers with activities led by local experts within Airbnb’s broader travel platform. It competes directly for the experience purchase and supports parts of group coordination.

##### Key Offerings

- Scheduled local experiences.
- Private-group bookings where offered by the host.
- Potential customization through direct host contact.
- Post-booking guest invitations, reservation sharing, and group messaging.

**Source:** [Airbnb group-booking details](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2594)

##### Pricing

| Component | Known Model |
|---|---|
| **Experience price** | Varies by listing. |
| **Private groups** | Host-set minimum; groups pay that minimum or the higher amount calculated for attendees. |
| **Platform commission** | Typically 20% deducted from the experience host’s payout. |
| **Guest service fee** | Airbnb currently states no additional guest service fee for experiences. |
| **Group discounts** | No universal group discount established. |

**Sources:** [Private-group pricing](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2595) · [Host commission](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3164) · [Guest fees](https://www.airbnb.ca/help/article/1604)

#### 2. Understand the Battlefield

##### Key Strengths

- **Direct offering overlap:** Local-expert-led experiences weaken “curated local experiences” as a standalone Roamly differentiator.
- **Existing travel context:** Customers already using Airbnb may prefer to keep activities in the same environment.
- **Established group features:** Private bookings, guest invitations, and shared messages address real coordinator needs.
- **Experience appeal:** A preferred activity, host, time slot, or price can outweigh coordination convenience.

##### Key Weaknesses

- **Pre-booking coordination opening:** The documented guest invitation and shared-message flow begins after confirmation.
- **Variable private-group availability:** Hosts decide whether to offer private bookings.
- **Minimum-price exposure:** Smaller private groups may face higher costs per attendee.
- **Payment uncertainty:** Split-payment availability must be checked for the specific experience and market. Do not claim it is universally absent.

**Sources:** [Group workflow and availability](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2594) · [Minimum-price rules](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2595)

#### 3. Win the Deal

##### Why You Win

- The customer already wants a Roamly experience.
- Coordinating participation and payments is blocking the booking.
- A shared booking and itinerary workflow reduces separate messages, records, and reimbursement tasks.
- The benefit is demonstrated through less coordinator effort or more completed group bookings.

##### Why They Win

- Airbnb has the preferred experience, date, capacity, or price.
- The group already uses Airbnb and values familiarity.
- One person is comfortable paying and collecting reimbursements.
- Roamly’s coordination benefits do not justify switching.

##### Why You

> **“You already trust Roamly for your own experiences. Roamly Groups helps you bring everyone along, align the plan, and split payments in the booking workflow.”**

**Best-fit customer:** An existing Roamly customer actively trying to organize a shared experience.

##### Defensibility

**Features alone are not a durable defense.** Split payments and itinerary sharing can be copied.

A stronger advantage would combine:

- Reliable group-ready inventory in Roamly’s cities.
- A participant workflow people consistently complete.
- Existing customer relationships at the moment of group intent.
- Evidence of higher booking completion, fewer payment reminders, and repeat coordinator use.

**Evidence status:** These are advantages to build and validate, not proven comparative outcomes.

#### 4. Handle the Hard Moments

##### Quick Dismiss

> “If everyone has agreed and one person is happy to book and collect reimbursements, Airbnb may meet your needs. Roamly Groups is most useful when organizing agreement and payments is what’s stopping the booking.”

##### Landmine Questions

| Ask | What It Reveals |
|---|---|
| “Before anyone books, how will you confirm who is actually joining?” | Commitment versus interest. |
| “For this exact experience, can each person pay their share, and when is the booking confirmed?” | Actual payment and confirmation workflow. |
| “If someone drops out or doesn’t pay, what happens to the booking and everyone else’s cost?” | Financial exposure and cancellation responsibility. |
| “Is a private group available, and what happens to the price if fewer people join?” | Availability and minimum-price implications. |
| “How many separate tools and messages will you need to keep the plan current?” | Value of integrated coordination. |

**Internal requirement:** Roamly must answer the same payment, cancellation, and commitment questions clearly.

##### Objection Handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| **“Airbnb already supports groups.”** | “Yes. The comparison is whether Roamly makes aligning participation and splitting payments before confirmation meaningfully easier.” |
| **“Airbnb has the experience we want.”** | “If Roamly cannot offer a suitable experience at the right time and price, Airbnb is the better choice.” |
| **“We already use Airbnb for the trip.”** | “That is a good reason to stay there. Roamly earns the extra step only if its experience or workflow saves enough effort.” |
| **“I can pay and collect reimbursements.”** | “You can. Split payments matter when collecting and tracking money creates a burden for you.” |
| **“Airbnb is cheaper.”** | “Compare the actual total, inclusions, and cancellation terms. We have no evidence Roamly is consistently cheaper.” |
| **“What if someone doesn’t pay or cancels?”** | “Review Roamly’s exact payment deadlines, confirmation rules, and cancellation terms before assuming the coordinator avoids financial exposure.” |

#### Messaging Guardrails

**Use:**

> “Get your group aligned, split the payments, and book a local experience together.”

**Avoid unsupported claims:**

- “Airbnb cannot do groups.”
- “Airbnb has no split payments.”
- “Roamly is cheaper.”
- “The coordinator never has to front money.”

## 4. Strategic bet

### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** the coordinator remains primary target, though group members benefit too.
- **The one thing worth choosing it for:** Let group members choose and revise their experiences within a shared plan before booking, reducing the coordinator’s work when preferences or attendance change.
- **The biggest threat to that position:** Airbnb could copy the flexibility features, so the features alone may not provide a lasting advantage

### Insight
The coordinator’s burden comes from having to administer everyone else’s participation. Roamly could reduce that burden by letting each member manage their own participation within a shared plan.

### Belief
I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when coordinators can be idea and experience connectors, because members managing their own participation frees coordinators to bring friends together with far less administrative work.

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by making it simple for a coordinator to propose an experience and invite others, then letting each participant join, select their options, and pay for their own selections.
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** To make this bet, I am deprioritizing professional travel organizers and corporate groups, whose workflows and commercial needs would require a different approach. I’ll focus first on individual travelers bringing friends together for shared local experiences.
