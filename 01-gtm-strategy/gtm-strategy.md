## 1. Discover Framework

### Align on the Goal
- **Core business problem:** Retain the users of group bookings and sessions
- **What success looks like:** Roamly supports a whole new type of customer, group travel. This might include a leader/coordinator type role and attendee type roles. This could bring in new customers, while retaining current customers. That implies extending the brand awareness to a wider audience. By accommodating multiple modes of travel, Roamly starts to become an app used widely for all types of travelors.

### Do the Research
- **Signals of a real opportunity:** Our customer support team has flagged this need. I don't have any numbers to justify the effort, so that should be an explicit follow up
- **Top 5 possible segments:** Personal/individual travel coordinators/agents, educational travel groups, senior travel groups, Conference attendees, wedding/event groups
- **How to size the opportunity:** How did we identify this customer type? How many individuals actually try to coordinate a group. Where in the process do they stop? Do they remain as a user for their own use after abandoning a group attempt. How many more "groups" might exist, how many of each type might exist, how often do they travel, and what other systems might they use

### Build a Working Team
- **Cross-functional stakeholders:** customer support, market analysts/research, marketing, and sales ops/sales

### Form Your Hypothesis
- **Most valuable segment to target first, and why:** Individual travel "coordinators" - these could be any of the "group" representatives, and could be our best customers (loyal enough to use Roamly for themselves, for a group, and continue with us after the group attempt doesn't work out)
- **Core value proposition:** For travelers coordinating group experiences, Roamly Groups makes it easier to align everyone’s plans and split payments, so they can enjoy the same local experiences together without manually reconciling who owes what.

## 4. The assumption I'm pushing back on

In general I agree with all the feedback, as this is an early proposal based on limited information. The "work" has not been completed to fully flesh out the opportunity. That said, the one assumption I would push back on is the coordinators pain will translate into group adoption. Today, this population isn't seeing completion of the travel bookings. By making the coordinators job easier, we will gain further insight into where future pain points exist and iteratively make it even easier. 

Also, this population is already trying to make the system work the way they want it to, so aligning the workflow to how they want it to work won't require much "learning"

## 5. V1 GTM strategy one-pager

Your pushback changes the launch framing: this is an effort to help customers complete a booking they are already trying to make. That supports starting with existing demand and learning through completed group journeys. Familiarity should reduce learning effort; whether the revised workflow removes the actual booking obstacle remains a hypothesis to test.

**Roamly Groups — V1 GTM Strategy**

**Business problem and opportunity**  
Some customers attempt to organize group travel through Roamly but do not complete their bookings. Customer support has flagged the need; the volume, abandonment causes, and commercial impact still need validation. Roamly Groups will make coordination and payment easier, while helping us identify the remaining obstacles.

**Initial target customer**  
Existing Roamly users acting as the informal coordinator for friends or family booking a shared local experience. Prioritize users with support requests or observable group-booking attempts. Treat educational, senior, conference, and wedding groups as research candidates for later prioritization.

**Value proposition**  
For travelers organizing a group experience, Roamly Groups brings shared planning, coordinated booking, and split payments into one flow—making it easier to get everyone booked and enjoy the experience together.

**Primary goal**  
Expand booking activity among existing customers by converting more attempted group bookings into completed bookings. Treat attendee acquisition and longer-term retention as secondary outcomes to investigate.

**GTM motion and recommended channels**  
Use a focused, product-led rollout to customers already showing group-booking intent.

- **In-product discovery:** Introduce Groups at relevant planning and booking steps, with a clear “Plan with a group” entry point.
- **Support-assisted recruitment:** Invite customers who reported group-booking friction into an initial pilot. Use their feedback to identify unresolved obstacles.
- **Targeted lifecycle email:** Re-engage customers with known group intent once the feature can address their reported problem.
- **Group invitations:** Make it easy for coordinators to bring attendees into the booking flow. Measure participation before treating invitations as a repeatable acquisition channel.

Defer broad paid awareness until the initial audience and completion benefit are clearer.

**Suggested primary success metric**  
**Group-booking completion rate:** the percentage of eligible group-booking attempts that result in a confirmed booking within a defined window.

Establish the baseline and booking window before launch. Compare pilot users with a comparable cohort—or use a randomized rollout if feasible—and set an improvement target after assessing baseline volume. Define what counts as an attempt and a completed group booking consistently.

Supporting diagnostics: abandonment by step, attendee invitation-to-participation rate, and group-related support contacts per attempt.

**Next steps**

1. Review support cases and booking behavior to quantify attempted group bookings and identify the leading failure points.
2. Recruit a small cohort of existing coordinators and observe how they organize, invite, and pay.
3. Launch the smallest workflow that addresses the leading obstacle; use completion data and feedback to prioritize the next iteration.

**Key hypothesis:** Customers already trying to coordinate group bookings will complete more bookings when Roamly supports their existing workflow. The first rollout should establish where that hypothesis holds and what still prevents completion.
